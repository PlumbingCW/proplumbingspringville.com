Water Meter Leak Test: How to Find a Hidden Leak in Springville, UT

To test for a hidden leak, turn off every tap and appliance, read your water meter, wait two hours without using any water and read it again. If the reading changed or the small flow indicator is still spinning, water is leaking somewhere past the meter.

This guide walks through the test, the common sources it points to and how to narrow the leak down before you call a plumber.

The two hour test

You need a flashlight, a pen and about two hours.

Stop all water use

Turn off taps, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker. Make sure nobody flushes a toilet. Pause any sprinkler or irrigation use.

Read the meter

Lift the meter lid. Note the numbers on the register. Many meters also have a small triangle or wheel that spins when any water flows. Write the reading down.

Wait two hours

Stay away from every water fixture during that time.

Read it again

If the numbers moved, or the small indicator moves even with everything off, there is a leak on your side of the meter.

What the result means

A moving meter with no use means water is leaving the plumbing system somewhere. The leak could be inside the house, in the yard or in the service line from the meter to the house. A steady meter means no leak in that period, though a leak that only runs under pressure at certain times may need a longer test.

Spanish Fork, a neighbor, reports that the city is responsible for the water line up to the first fitting after the meter. Springville's Water Division at 801-489-2740 can tell you where its responsibility ends for your address. Anything past that point is the homeowner's to find and fix.

Springville irrigation season and your meter

Springville runs a pressurized irrigation system separate from drinking water. In 2024 the city began charging the system on April 8 and asked residents to wait until April 15 to use it. Irrigation leaks and open valves in spring can show up as sudden water use that looks like a house leak.

If the meter test fails in spring, shut off the irrigation supply and repeat. If the meter stops, the problem is in the sprinkler system. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the house plumbing or the service line. The Water Division at 801-489-2740 can answer questions about how your lot is served.

The toilet dye test

Toilets are the most common source of silent leaks. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, do not flush and wait about 15 minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper or the fill valve is leaking. A worn flapper is a cheap part, and fixing it can cut a surprising amount of waste.

Run the test on every toilet in the house. A fill valve that lets water trickle into the overflow tube is another cause. If you hear a faint hiss or a toilet refills on its own, check the flapper first.

Closing the main to narrow it down

If the meter still moves with all fixtures off, close the main shutoff valve inside the house and look at the meter again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house, which usually means a buried line problem.

Buried line leaks may show as a soggy patch in the yard, a drop in pressure or an unexpected green strip in the lawn. Our leak detection page explains how we find them, and water line repair covers fixing the line.

A note on gas meters

A gas meter dial that moves with all appliances off can also point to a leak, but gas is different. If you smell gas, leave the building, avoid switches and call 911 and your gas utility from outside. Enbridge Gas lists 1-800-767-1689 for gas emergencies in Utah. Never test for a gas leak with a flame.

When to call

Call when the test shows a leak you cannot find, when the water bill jumps without explanation or when you see damp walls, mold smells or a wet spot in the yard. Hidden leaks do damage quietly, and finding them early costs less.

We also dispatch to Provo and Mapleton, so the same advice applies on the same day if you live outside Springville.

Reading different meter styles

Older meters use a sweep hand and a row of digits, like a car odometer. Newer ones may show a digital display. Many have a leak indicator, a small triangle or star that turns when any water passes. If the indicator turns slowly, the leak is small. If it spins quickly, water is moving fast, and the leak may be large.

If you cannot read your meter or cannot find the lid, ask the Water Division at 801-489-2740. Do not pry the lid with a screwdriver in freezing weather, since the lid can crack. Keep the area clear of debris and soil so the meter stays accessible.

After the repair

Once the leak is fixed, run the meter test again to confirm it. Keep the first reading in a notebook so you have a baseline. A quick check each season, especially before irrigation starts, catches new leaks early.

Code and permit note

Per the International Plumbing Code (IPC), pipes are pressure tested after installation, which is the same idea as a meter test for an old system.
