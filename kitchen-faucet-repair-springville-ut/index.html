Kitchen Faucet Repair: Fix a Drip or Leak in Springville, UT

Most kitchen faucet drips come from a worn cartridge, washer, O ring or seat, and you can usually fix them in under an hour with a few tools. The key is to identify the type of faucet, shut off the water and replace the exact part that wears out.

This guide covers the four common faucet types, a simple repair sequence and the signs it is time to replace the faucet instead.

The repair depends on the mechanism inside the handle.

A basic repair sequence

These steps work for most faucets. Check your model for details.

Shut off the water

Close the shutoff valves under the sink. Open the faucet to release pressure. Plug the drain so small parts do not fall in.

Remove the handle

Pry off the decorative cap, remove the screw and lift off the handle. Some models use a set screw.

Pull the cartridge or parts

Take out the retaining nut, then the cartridge, disc or washers. Photograph each step so reassembly is easy.

Replace and reassemble

Match the part exactly, replace O rings and reassemble. Turn the water on slowly and test.

Springville water and your faucet

Springville City Water System draws from four springs and seven wells, and the city's 2024 report lists total dissolved solids from 240 to 476 ppm without a hardness figure. Mineral buildup can clog aerators and score faucet seats, so a sputtering stream often means a scaled aerator rather than a failing faucet.

Unscrew the aerator, soak it in vinegar and clean it with an old toothbrush. If scale returns quickly, test your tap with a kit or ask the Water Division at 801-489-2740. The same test helps you decide if a water softener makes sense.

Where leaks hide

A drip from the spout points to a worn cartridge or washer. Water at the base of the handle means worn O rings. Water at the base of the faucet, around the deck, can mean a loose mounting nut or a cracked body. Water under the sink may come from supply lines or shutoff valves instead of the faucet at all.

Dry the area, wrap tissue around each connection and run the faucet. The wet spot shows you where to look. Replacing a supply line is cheap, and braided stainless lines last longer than thin plastic ones.

When to replace the faucet

If the faucet is old, pitted or corroded, repair parts may cost nearly as much as a new faucet. A cracked body cannot be repaired. Mineral damage to the seats in a worn compression faucet may keep it dripping even with new washers.

Replacement is also a chance to upgrade to a pull down sprayer or a single handle model. Check the number of holes in your sink and the deck space before buying. Our faucet repair page covers what we handle, and plumbing installation covers new fixtures.

Do not ignore shutoff valves

If the shutoff valves under the sink will not close or leak when moved, stop turning them and call. An old valve can fail when turned. Replacing it is a short job, and it makes every later repair safer.

Cartridge replacement in more detail

Single handle faucets often use a cartridge that slides out after you remove a retaining clip or nut. Before pulling it, note the orientation of the cartridge, since many have a notch or a flat side that must line up with the body. Pull straight up, twisting gently if needed. A cartridge puller tool helps with stubborn ones.

Wipe the faucet body clean, apply plumber's grease to the new O rings and slide the new cartridge into the same orientation. Tighten the retaining nut snugly but not hard. Turn the handle through its full range before turning on water to make sure it moves freely.

Compression faucets and seats

Compression faucets use a rubber washer that presses on a seat inside the valve. When the washer hardens or the seat gets scored, water drips past. Replace the washer and, if the seat is rough, resurface it with a seat dresser or replace it. Many older faucets are worth replacing because parts can be hard to find.

Do not overtighten the handle to stop a drip. That pushes the washer into the seat and wears it faster, so the drip comes back worse.

Supply lines and shutoff valves

The flexible hoses that connect the faucet to the shutoff valves wear out. Replace them when you replace the faucet, since they are cheap and often the part that fails. Hand tighten and then use a wrench for a quarter turn. Check each connection for drips after turning the water on.

Tools to have on hand

An adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, a hex key set, plumber's grease, replacement O rings, a flashlight and a small container for parts cover most repairs. A basin wrench reaches the mounting nuts behind the sink. Take the old part to the hardware store to match it.

A quick troubleshooting order

If the faucet drips from the spout, start with the cartridge or washer. If the handle leaks, replace the O rings. If water appears under the sink, check supply lines and shutoffs first. If water pressure is weak, clean the aerator. If hot water is slow to arrive, the problem may be the water heater, not the faucet. Working in order saves parts and time.
