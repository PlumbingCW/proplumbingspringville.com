Emergency Leak Detection in Springville, UT

A hidden leak does damage you cannot see until a stain blooms on a ceiling or a water bill arrives twice the usual size. Leak emergency: active leak or soaking ceiling

Water coming through a ceiling, wall or floor is an emergency. Stop the source, protect what you can and call.

Shut off the main

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, call and we will help you locate the right shutoff.

Kill power near water

Switch off the breaker for rooms with wet ceilings, fixtures or outlets. Never touch a wet light fixture or outlet.

Open and drain

Poke a small hole in a ceiling bulge over a bucket to relieve water pressure and keep the damage from spreading. Photograph the damage first for your records.

Note what you see

Where the stain started, whether it grows with water use, and any hissing or running sounds help narrow the search before the visit.

The two minute meter check you can do now

A water meter that moves when nothing is running proves a leak exists somewhere on your side of it. It takes two minutes and costs nothing.

Turn everything off

Stop the dishwasher, washer, ice maker, softener cycle and any running fixture. Check that no toilet is refilling.

Find the leak indicator

Open the meter lid and look for the small triangle or dial that spins slowly with very low flow.

Wait and watch

If it turns with everything off, or the digits advance after a wait, water is escaping somewhere.

Tell us what you saw

Whether it moved, how fast and what time it was gives the technician a starting point.

Signs of a hidden leak

Leaks announce themselves in small ways long before a flood. These show up most often in calls.

High water bill

A bill that climbs while habits stay the same is the most common sign. A leaking toilet flapper alone can add many gallons a day.

Warm spot on the floor

A warm patch on a slab suggests a leak on a hot water line under the floor.

Sound of running water

Hissing or trickling when everything is off often comes from a pressurized line in a wall or under the slab.

Musty smell or stains

Damp odor, peeling paint or discolored drywall points to a slow, long running leak.

Low water pressure

Pressure that falls without a clear cause can come from a leak on the supply line.

Wet yard or meter box

Soggy patches, or water standing in the meter box, suggest a leak on the service line outside the house.

Slab, crawl space, yard and commercial leaks

Where a leak sits decides how it is found and fixed. Slab leaks usually need acoustic and thermal work and sometimes a rerouted line, while crawl space and wall leaks are often reached from below or through a small access hole.

Outdoor leaks on the line between meter and house are covered on our water line repair page, and a leak that has already burst a pipe moves to burst pipe repair. Commercial buildings add more fixtures and longer pipe runs, so we plan the test sequence before arriving.

Springville ground and your water line

Hobble Creek runs through the city and Utah Lake sits roughly three miles west, so groundwater and irrigation both matter here. A wet patch in a yard is not always a plumbing leak. Springville's pressurized irrigation system supplies outdoor water separately from the drinking water system, and in 2024 the city began charging it in early April and asked residents to wait until April 15 to run systems.

That timing is useful. If a spot of lawn goes soggy right after the irrigation turns on, a broken sprinkler line is a likely suspect. If it stays wet in January with irrigation off, a buried culinary line is worth testing, and the city's Water Division can be reached at 801-489-2740 about the meter side.

Code and permit note

Code note: after a repair, supply lines are pressure tested in the way the International Plumbing Code (IPC) expects for new work, so a fixed leak stays fixed.
