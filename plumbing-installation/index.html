Springville basement and permit notes

Springville's basement permit guide says converting an unfinished basement or adding plumbing needs an application and permit before work starts. It lists plan review at about three weeks and requires rough inspections before plumbing is covered, plus a final inspection before use.

The same guide lists common misses: nail plates for pipes within 1 1/4 inches of the stud face, 18 inches of access at cleanouts after drywall and proper pipe support. Call 801-489-2704 to confirm requirements for your project.

Questions to settle before work starts

Who supplies the fixtures, and what happens if one arrives damaged or with the wrong rough in dimension? Who pulls the permit, if one applies? When will inspections happen, and what must stay open until they pass?

We answer these in writing in plain language before starting. Clear scope is the cheapest way to avoid a change order halfway through a remodel, and it keeps schedules honest for everyone on the job.

Working around an occupied home

Most plumbing installation happens while people live in the house. We protect floors, shut water off only when needed and tell you before each shutoff. Cutting into walls is planned so patching stays small.

If you are remodeling, we coordinate with your other trades. Plumbing rough in goes in before insulation and drywall, and fixtures go in after paint, so timing matters. A short call before each phase keeps the schedule from slipping.

Keep fixture boxes, spec sheets and warranty cards. If a part fails years later, the model number saves time and money.

Check the license

Utah licenses apprentice, journeyman and master plumbers, including residential classes, through the Division of Professional Licensing. Any plumber should be willing to let you verify a license at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html.

Ask whether the work needs a permit, who pulls it and who schedules inspections. Our team can guide you, and the city's Building Division at 801-489-2704 has the final word.

Code and permit note

Code note: new plumbing is inspected against the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as Utah amends it, including rough in checks before walls close.
