Commercial plumbing emergency: what to do now

A business has more people and more fixtures at risk. These steps protect the building and the public.

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff or the branch valve for the affected area. Know where both are before you need them.

Secure the area

Block off wet floors, keep customers out and cover floor drains if a backup is rising.

Protect electrical and stock

Move inventory and equipment off the floor and keep power off near water.

Document and call

Photograph the damage for insurance and call the 24/7 line. Tell us the building type so we send the right equipment.

Problems we see in commercial buildings

Commercial plumbing carries heavier use and longer runs than a home. The faults are familiar but larger.

Kitchen drain backups

Grease and food waste clog restaurant lines. Jetting and a grease control plan prevent repeat backups, see hydro jetting.

Restroom problems

Running toilets, slow urinals and clogged floor drains hit customers directly.

Water heater failures

A commercial heater that fails stops dishwashing and handwashing. Tank and tankless units are both serviced.

Hidden leaks

Long pipe runs in ceilings and slabs can leak for weeks. See leak detection.

Permits and codes for commercial work

Springville's Building Division publishes commercial and tenant improvement permit processes, and plumbing changes in a tenant space or a remodel can need a permit and inspection. Call 801-489-2704 and ask before work starts.

Utah licenses commercial plumbers, and you can verify any licensee at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html. Ask for it from anyone you hire.
