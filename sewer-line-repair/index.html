Sewer backup emergency: what to do first

A sewer backup that reaches the floor needs fast action and careful cleanup. The steps below protect you and limit what the water reaches.

Stop all water use

No showers, no flushing, no laundry. Each use adds water to a line that cannot carry it away.

Keep out of the water

Sewage carries bacteria. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and keep people and pets out of the affected room.

Turn off power if needed

If water has reached outlets, appliances or a panel, do not touch them. Switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Document it

Take photos for insurance before cleanup. Ask your insurer whether your policy covers sewer backup, since many exclude it unless endorsed.

How we diagnose and fix a sewer line

A repair is only as good as the diagnosis. We do not recommend digging until we have seen the pipe.

Clear the line

We open the blockage with a cable or jetter so water can drain and the camera can pass. See sewer line cleaning for the methods.

Camera the pipe

A camera on a push cable shows roots, a belly, offset joints, cracks or a collapse and records the depth and location.

Choose the repair

We lay out the options with the trade-offs and a price. Some pipes can be lined or patched. Others need a section replaced.

Repair and verify

After the repair we camera the line again and run water to confirm flow before restoring the work area.

Causes we see in sewer lines

Tree roots are a leading cause, because they find the smallest joint and keep growing. Grease and wipes build a slow blockage. Older materials like clay and cast iron crack and corrode, and ground movement can pull joints apart or put a sag in the line.

A camera shows which cause you have. That matters because lining a pipe that has collapsed wastes money, while digging a pipe that only needs lining costs more than it should. We cover the camera step in more detail on our sewer camera inspection page.

Springville rules and who owns what

In Springville, the sewer lateral is inspected as part of new construction, and the city's residential permit checklist lists the sewer lateral among the required inspections. Repair of an existing lateral can involve a permit and a city inspection, and the Building Division at 801-489-2704 can tell you what applies to your address.

Digging also means calling before you dig. The national 811 call before you dig line exists to have buried utilities marked, and a repair crew should have lines located before any trench opens. Ask any contractor you hire how and when they handle this.
