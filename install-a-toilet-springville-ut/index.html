How to Install a Toilet: Step by Step for Springville, UT Homes

Installing a toilet takes about two hours and four basics: a sound floor flange, a new wax ring, level bolts and a tight supply line. Most problems come from a damaged flange, a toilet that rocks or a rushed seal.

This guide lists the tools, walks through each step in order and explains when a plumber should take over.

Remove the old toilet

Prepare the space before lifting anything.

Shut off the water

Close the valve at the wall, flush and hold the handle to drain the tank. Sponge out the rest.

Disconnect the supply

Unscrew the supply line from the tank. Place a towel to catch drips.

Unbolt and lift

Remove the nuts at the base. Rock the toilet gently to break the old seal and lift straight up. Toilets are heavy, so ask for help.

Stuff a rag in the flange

This blocks sewer gas while you work. Scrape off the old wax from the flange.

Check the flange first

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to. It should sit on top of the finished floor, firmly attached. A cracked or corroded flange will not hold bolts, and the toilet will rock. A repair ring or a new flange fixes it.

If the flange sits below the floor surface after new tile was installed, you may need a flange extender or a thicker seal. A gap between the toilet and the floor is a leak waiting to happen.

Set the new toilet

Take your time on this part, since a toilet that is set crooked or twisted may leak later.

Install the bolts

Slide the closet bolts into the flange slots and position them straight up.

Place the wax ring

Set the ring on the flange or the toilet horn, as the maker directs.

Lower the toilet

Line the holes up with the bolts and press down firmly, without twisting. Check that it is level.

Tighten evenly

Tighten the nuts a little at a time on each side. Over tightening can crack the porcelain. Trim the bolts and fit the caps.

Local notes for Springville

Before you start a bathroom remodel in Springville, ask whether permits apply. The city's published information does not cover every project, so call the Building Division at 801-489-2704. The city also lists inspections for the sewer lateral, which matters if you are replacing drains and not only the toilet.

Springville's water comes from four springs and seven wells, and the 2024 report lists total dissolved solids from 240 to 476 ppm. Mineral deposits can clog the small holes under the rim of a toilet over time, so if a toilet flushes weakly in a home with mineral buildup, check the rim jets.

Connect the water and test

Attach the tank if it is separate, using the washers and bolts as instructed. Connect the supply line and open the valve slowly. Let the tank fill, then flush several times. Check the base and the supply connection for drips, and wipe a dry paper towel around them to catch small leaks.

Run a dye test after a day: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and see if color shows in the bowl, which means the flapper leaks. Finally, caulk around the base, leaving a small gap at the back if your local practice calls for it so leaks can show.

Common problems after installation

A rocking toilet needs shims. A slow fill can come from a partially open valve. A toilet that will not flush well might have a blocked rim or a misadjusted flapper chain. A drip at the floor means the seal failed, so lift the toilet and check the flange and the ring.

If a toilet keeps clogging, the problem may be in the drain, and a plumber should look at it. See toilet repair and installation for what we handle and drain cleaning for clogs beyond the toilet.

Choosing a toilet

Most homes use a two piece toilet, with a separate tank and bowl, in a round or elongated shape. Elongated bowls are more comfortable but need more space. Check the flush rating and look for a model with a tall bowl if comfort matters. Measure the space and the rough in distance before buying.

Dual flush and pressure assisted models have their own installation notes, so read the instructions. Keep the old toilet rough in measurement handy at the store. A smaller or low profile tank may not fit a tight space, and a taller one may hit a shelf. Taking a minute to measure saves a return trip to the store.

When to hire a plumber

Call when the flange is damaged, when the floor around the toilet is soft or stained, when you are moving a toilet to a new spot or when you are not comfortable lifting it. Water damage under a toilet can hide for months. A plumber can also check the shutoff valve and replace it if it sticks.

Code and permit note

Toilet installation follows International Plumbing Code (IPC) fixture rules, including a vented drain and a properly sealed flange.
