Types of Plumbing Pipes in Springville, UT Homes

Most Springville homes use a handful of pipe materials: copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized steel for water supply, and PVC, ABS and cast iron for drains. You can usually tell which you have by color, feel and where the pipe runs, and the material decides how it fails and when to replace it.

This guide explains each type in plain language, shows how to identify what is in your walls and points out which problems call for a plumber.

Pipe materials at a glance

Supply pipes carry pressurized clean water. Drain pipes carry waste by gravity. Mixing them up is the most common mistake in online advice, so keep the two groups separate.

Supply pipes: copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized

Copper has a long track record and handles heat well, but it can develop pinhole leaks and its rigid joints can split in a hard freeze. PEX bends around corners with fewer fittings and tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe, but it should never sit in direct sunlight and needs proper fittings.

CPVC was popular for hot and cold lines and is rigid like copper. With time and heat it can turn brittle, so a repair that looks easy may crack the next section. Galvanized steel is the oldest of the group. It rusts from the inside, narrows over the years and lowers pressure long before it leaks.

Drain pipes: PVC, ABS and cast iron

PVC and ABS are common in newer drains and vents. They resist corrosion and are light, which is why they are easy to cut and cement. They can crack if struck or if water freezes inside, so exposed runs in cold spaces need protection.

Cast iron drains are heavy, quiet and long lived, but they rust from the inside and can crack with age. A slow drain that returns quickly after cleaning can point to scale or a damaged section, and a camera inspection will show which. See our sewer camera inspection page for how that works.

How to tell what pipe you have

A few minutes with a flashlight answers most questions.

Find the water entry

Look at the main line where it enters the house, usually in a basement, crawl space or utility room. The material at the shutoff tells you what feeds the house.

Check color and feel

Reddish metal is copper, gray threaded metal is galvanized steel and flexible colored tube is PEX.

Use a magnet

A magnet sticks to galvanized steel and cast iron but not to copper or plastic.

Look for markings

Plastic pipe usually has letters printed along its length that name the type.

What the Springville water report says

Springville City Water System draws from four springs and seven wells, and its 2024 report lists total dissolved solids between 240 and 476 ppm. The report does not state hardness. Water chemistry can affect how fast scale builds inside pipe and fixtures, so a home test kit or a call to the Water Division at 801-489-2740 is a sensible first step before you blame the pipe.

If you see brown water from the tap, run the cold water for a minute. Brown water that clears quickly often comes from stirred sediment, while water that stays rusty points toward your own steel pipes or tank.

When replacement makes sense

One leak does not mean replace everything. Repeated pinhole leaks, rusty water on every tap, falling pressure or known problem material all suggest it is time. In that case, see repiping, which explains PEX and copper options and what the project involves.

For a single damaged section, a spot repair is fine. For a broken line from the street to the house, see water line repair. For a hidden leak you cannot locate, leak detection finds it without opening walls.

Permits and licensing

Whether a pipe replacement needs a permit depends on the scope. Springville's published FAQ does not cover it, so call the Building Division at 801-489-2704. The city's permit checklist lists inspections for underground plumbing, sewer laterals and rough plumbing, scheduled through its City Inspect portal with 24 hours of notice.

Utah licenses plumbers as apprentices, journeymen and master plumbers. You can look up any license at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html before hiring.

Mistakes we see when pipes are mixed

Mixing metals can cause trouble. Connecting copper directly to galvanized steel without the right fitting lets the two metals corrode each other where they meet, a reaction called galvanic corrosion. A dielectric union or a proper transition fitting breaks that path.

Another mistake is gluing the wrong plastic to the wrong cement. PVC, ABS and CPVC each use their own solvent cement, and a joint made with the wrong one can fail months later. Cast iron needs rubber couplings with stainless bands, not glue.

Finally, avoid hiding a repair behind a wall without a pressure test. A joint that seems fine at low pressure can fail when the system is under full pressure, and the damage stays hidden until the drywall stains.

Neighbors face the same questions

Homes in Mapleton and Spanish Fork are a mix of older and newer, so the same identification steps apply there. Fast growth in both towns means many homes have modern plastic supply lines, while older cores may still have steel.

Code and permit note

Pipe materials for water and drains are listed as approved or not under the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Utah applies with statewide amendments.
