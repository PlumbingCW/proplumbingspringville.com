Sewer Camera Inspection in Springville, UT

A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside the pipe without digging. Sewer camera inspection in Springville from Springville Pro Plumbing finds roots, breaks, sags and buildup, and it gives you a recorded look at the line.

It is useful before a home purchase, after repeated backups and before any repair decision.

What the camera shows

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels through the line to the city main. It shows pipe material, joints, roots, cracks, offsets, bellies where water sits and any buildup. A locator can mark the surface above any defect and give a depth.

The footage turns a guess into evidence. Instead of paying for a repair based on a symptom, you see what is wrong and how far down the line it is.

How an inspection goes

Most inspections take about an hour of on site work, depending on access.

Find access

We use an exterior cleanout, or pull a toilet if no cleanout exists.

Run the camera

We feed the camera through the line and record while we watch.

Locate defects

We mark and measure the location and depth of anything we find.

Explain

We review the footage with you and explain what it means and what, if anything, to do.

Why it matters before a home purchase

A sewer line problem found after closing is the buyer's problem. A camera inspection before closing gives you a record, a repair estimate and leverage in negotiations. It costs little compared to a line replacement.

Springville's city requires inspection of the sewer lateral on new construction according to its permit checklist, but older lines have no such guarantee. Ask the Building Division at 801-489-2704 about requirements for any repair you plan.

Reading the video together

After the run, we pause on anything unusual and explain it in plain terms. A single stained joint is common and not a crisis. Roots filling a third of the pipe diameter or a visible gap is a different story.

We also note the pipe material, because clay, cast iron, concrete and plastic age differently. Knowing what is in the ground helps you judge whether a repair now is smarter than waiting, and it gives a future plumber a map of the line.

Prepare for the visit

Locate your cleanout before we arrive if you can. It is usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard. Tell us about past backups, slow drains and any earlier repairs, since each detail narrows what the camera should look for.

Keep pets away from the work area and clear a path to the access point. If a toilet must be pulled for access, we reset it with a new wax ring afterward.

Planned inspection versus emergency

An inspection done on a schedule is cheaper than one during a backup. If the line is already backing up, the line must be cleared first, which is an emergency call. See our emergency pages for that.

Homes served by sewer systems of the area benefit from a baseline look every few years when trees are close to the line.

Code and permit note

Code note: camera footage helps show whether a lateral meets the International Plumbing Code (IPC) basics for slope and joints.
