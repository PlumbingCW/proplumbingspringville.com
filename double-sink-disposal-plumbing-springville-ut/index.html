Double Sink and Garbage Disposal Plumbing in Springville, UT

A double bowl kitchen sink with a disposal drains through a continuous waste pipe that links both bowls, then through the disposal outlet and a P-trap to the wall. The disposal goes under one bowl, the other bowl drains through a tailpiece into the same line, and the dishwasher hose connects to the disposal or to a high loop.

This guide explains each part, the order they connect in and the mistakes that cause slow drains and leaks.

Layout, step by step

Start at the bowls. One bowl holds the disposal. The other bowl has a basket strainer and a tailpiece that drops to a horizontal pipe called the continuous waste. That pipe runs to the side outlet on the disposal, so both bowls share one trap.

From the disposal outlet, a short horizontal pipe runs to the P-trap and then to the wall stub. The trap arm needs a downward slope toward the wall, and the vent for the line is in the wall. A trap that sits too low or an arm that slopes the wrong way holds water and drains slowly.

Connecting the dishwasher

Dishwasher drain water must not flow back into the sink.

Remove the knockout

New disposals have a plug in the dishwasher inlet. Knock it out from the inside and fish out the plug. Skipping this step is the most common cause of a dishwasher that will not drain.

Connect the hose

Attach the dishwasher drain hose to the inlet with a clamp.

Make a high loop

Run the hose up to the underside of the countertop and secure it, then down to the inlet. The loop keeps sink water from backing into the dishwasher.

Test

Run the dishwasher and check for leaks at the hose, the inlet and the trap.

Why this matters in a Springville kitchen

Springville homes range from older houses to newer subdivisions, and the sink layouts vary just as much. Older kitchens may have two separate traps and a tee, while newer builds use a continuous waste. In winter, with a January mean low near 22 degrees Fahrenheit, grease sticks to cold pipes faster, so what goes down the disposal in December can build up sooner than in July.

If you are remodeling and moving the sink, remember that the city may require a permit when plumbing changes. The city's published information does not cover every case, so call the Building Division at 801-489-2704 to ask.

Mistakes that cause slow drains and leaks

Using two traps on one sink, one per bowl, is a common old setup that invites trouble. Another mistake is using flexible corrugated trap pieces, which collect debris in their ridges. A smooth walled trap drains better.

Over tightening plastic slip nuts can crack them, while under tightening lets them drip. Hand tight plus a quarter turn is the usual rule. Also avoid putty on rubber washers where the maker says not to use it. Read the instructions that come with your disposal.

Choosing and sizing a disposal

Disposals are rated by motor horsepower, and a larger motor handles more waste with less jamming. Continuous feed models start when you flip a switch, while batch feed models run only when the stopper is turned in place. Batch feed is safer around children but slower to use.

Check the mounting style as well. Quick lock mounts make replacement easier. Make sure the new unit lines up with the existing trap arm height, or you will need extra fittings. A mismatch between the disposal outlet and the wall stub is a common reason a simple swap turns into a bigger job. Our plumbing installation page explains the kind of plan we make for such projects.

Air gaps and local code

Some jurisdictions require an air gap, a small fitting on the countertop that breaks any direct connection between the dishwasher drain and the sink drain. Others accept a high loop. Springville's published information does not say which applies to your kitchen, so ask the Building Division at 801-489-2704 if you are remodeling.

Whichever method is used, the goal is the same: wastewater from the dishwasher must never flow backward into the dishwasher. If you ever see dirty water in the dishwasher after running the sink, check the loop first.

What not to put down the disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, eggshells in large amounts, pasta and rice, fibrous vegetable scraps and bones are poor choices. They collect in the trap and the line. Run cold water while the disposal runs and for a short time after to move scraps along.

If the disposal hums but does not spin, it is jammed. Turn off power at the switch, use the hex socket at the bottom to turn the blades back and forth and press the reset button. If it keeps jamming, see garbage disposal repair.

When to call a plumber

Call when a leak keeps returning, when both bowls back up together or when the drain smells of sewer. A trap that gurgles points to a vent problem. A backup that affects other fixtures points to the main line, covered on our drain cleaning page.

Code and permit note

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) addresses sink traps, vents and dishwasher drain connections, so a continuous waste layout is a code matter and not only a style.
