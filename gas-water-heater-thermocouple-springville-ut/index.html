Gas Water Heater Pilot Light and Thermocouple: Springville, UT Guide

A thermocouple is a small safety sensor that tells a gas water heater the pilot flame is lit. If the pilot keeps going out, a worn thermocouple is a common cause, but a gas smell is a different matter: leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility first.

This guide covers what the part does, how to relight a pilot safely and when to stop and call a plumber.

What the thermocouple does

The thermocouple sits in the pilot flame and generates a tiny electric current when hot. That current holds the gas valve open. If the flame goes out, the current stops and the valve closes, so gas does not flow into the house.

Over time the tip can get coated with soot or wear out. Then it cannot generate enough current, so the valve closes even when the flame is lit, and the pilot seems to go out on its own.

Signs the thermocouple is the problem

These symptoms often point to the part, but a plumber should confirm.

Pilot lights then goes out

You hold the button, it lights, then fails when you release it.

Weak or yellow flame

A pilot flame should be steady and mostly blue. A lazy flame can mean dirt or a clogged tube.

Dark soot at the tip

Soot insulates the sensor and stops it heating.

How to relight a pilot safely

Always follow the label on your own heater. These steps match most models.

Turn the control to pilot

Set the gas control knob to pilot and wait the time the label specifies so any gas clears.

Light the pilot

Press and hold the knob while lighting the pilot with the igniter or a long lighter.

Hold the button

Keep holding for the time shown on the label so the thermocouple heats.

Release and set to on

If the pilot stays lit, turn the knob to on and set the temperature.

Cold weather in Springville

Springville's January mean low is about 22 degrees Fahrenheit, and a gas heater in an unheated garage works harder in winter. Drafts that blow out a pilot are more common when doors open in cold weather. Keep combustion air open and never block the vent area with stored items.

Other reasons a pilot goes out

A weak or blocked pilot tube starves the flame. Dust, lint and spider webs are common culprits in garages and basements. A draft from a door or an exhaust fan can blow out the flame. A failing gas control valve can also close early and mimic a bad thermocouple.

Low gas pressure can do the same. If other gas appliances in the house also act up, call your gas utility, since the problem may be outside your equipment. Do not adjust gas pressure yourself.

Cleaning around the burner

With the gas control set to off and the heater cool, a vacuum with a brush attachment can remove dust near the burner access panel. Do not poke tools into the pilot tube or burner ports. If the flame still looks wrong after cleaning, stop and call a plumber.

Keep stored items away from the heater, including paint, solvents and gasoline. Fumes can ignite from a pilot flame. Combustion air needs room to flow, and the vent must stay clear.

Annual checks worth doing

Once a year, look at the flame, test the temperature and pressure relief valve by lifting its lever briefly, check around the base for moisture and flush a few gallons from the drain valve to clear sediment. If you are not comfortable with any step, a technician can do them in a short visit.

Also check that the carbon monoxide alarm near the heater works. Gas appliances produce combustion gases, and a working vent and alarm are your protection.

Why a technician is worth it for gas work

Gas appliances combine fuel, ignition and exhaust in one machine. A mistake can leak gas or send carbon monoxide into the house. A technician checks the flame, the vent draft, the relief valve and the gas connections in the same visit, and can tell you if the heater is near the end of its life.

If you decide to try a relight yourself, follow the label, never force a knob and stop at the first sign of a gas odor. A heater that needs repeated relights is telling you something is wrong.

When replacement of the part is not enough

A new thermocouple costs little compared with the heater, but a tank that leaks at the base cannot be saved. See water heater repair for diagnosis and water heater replacement when the tank has failed. Rusting at the base, water pooling or a tank past its expected life point to replacement.

Gas lines and permits

Springville's permit checklist lists a gas line inspection scheduled through its City Inspect portal with 24 hours of notice. If your work changes gas piping, ask the Building Division at 801-489-2704 whether a permit applies.

Code and permit note

Gas water heaters are installed under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the maker's instructions, including venting and combustion air.
