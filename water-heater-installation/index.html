Choosing the right water heater for your home

Start with how your household uses hot water. A family with several showers at the same time needs a different unit than one or two people. Tank capacity, recovery rate and fuel type all matter.

Gas is common in Utah homes and heats quickly. Electric units are simpler and need no venting. Heat pump models use less electricity but need air space and a drain. Tankless units heat on demand and need strong gas or electric supply. We talk through these before recommending anything.

What the installation includes

A good installation is more than swapping the tank. These steps matter for safety and for how long the unit lasts.

Site check

We check the location, venting, gas or electrical supply and space around the unit.

Set and connect

We set the unit, connect water with new flexible connectors and fit shutoff valves.

Safety devices

We install a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line and an expansion tank where needed.

Start up and test

We fire or energize the unit, check combustion and venting on gas models, and show you the controls.

Permits and licensing in Springville

Whether your installation needs a permit depends on the scope. Springville's published FAQ does not say, so call the Building Division at 801-489-2704 and ask. The city schedules required inspections through its City Inspect portal with 24 hours of notice.

Utah licenses plumbers in apprentice, journeyman and master classes, including residential classes. You can verify any plumber, including us, at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html before you hire.

Sizing and placement questions we answer first

Before quoting, we ask how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms run at once and whether there is a soaking tub or a large kitchen. A unit that is too small runs out. A unit that is too large costs more to buy and more to run.

We also look at where the heater sits. A garage unit needs protection from vehicles and clearance for service. A basement unit needs a drain nearby in case of a leak. An attic or closet unit needs a drain pan with a line to a safe termination, because a leak there damages ceilings below.

Gas units need combustion air and a vent that slopes and seals correctly. If the old vent was sized for a different appliance, we say so and quote the change instead of reusing a bad vent. Safety work is never an optional extra.

Water quality and longevity

Springville City Water System draws from four springs and seven wells. Its 2024 report lists total dissolved solids from 240 to 476 ppm and no hardness value. Scale in a water heater depends on hardness, so test your tap with a kit, or ask the Water Division at 801-489-2740 for guidance.

If you find hard water, consider a water softener and a flush schedule for the tank. If you would like on demand hot water, see our tankless water heater page.

Code and permit note

Code note: new water heaters are installed to International Plumbing Code (IPC) requirements, which Utah applies with statewide amendments.
