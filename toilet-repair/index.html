Toilet emergency: stop the water first

The steps are quick and need no tools. Doing them before we arrive keeps a bathroom problem from becoming a ceiling problem.

Close the supply valve

Turn the valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. This ends the refill and prevents more water reaching the bowl.

Lift the float

If the bowl is still rising, take off the tank lid and hold the flapper closed or lift the float so no more water enters.

Do not flush again

Flushing a clogged toilet adds water the line cannot carry. Wait for the plumber or use a flange plunger gently once.

Protect the floor

Lay towels around the base. If a ceiling below is wet, call right away and mention it so the dispatcher can prioritize.

Common toilet problems and what they mean

Most toilet calls trace back to a handful of worn parts. A quick look inside the tank often shows which.

Runs on and off

A worn flapper or a fill valve that does not shut off lets water leak into the bowl. This wastes water and raises the bill.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets, a low tank water level or a partial drain clog reduce flush power. A cleaning or adjustment usually restores it.

Rocks or leaks at the base

A loose bolt, a failed wax ring or a cracked flange lets water seep under the toilet and rot the subfloor over time.

Sweating or dripping tank

Condensation is cosmetic, but drips from the tank bolts or supply line are leaks that need fixing.

Repair or replace the toilet

Many toilets only need a cheap part. Others are better replaced, especially older high volume models.

Repair when

  • The porcelain is intact
  • The problem is a flapper, fill valve or flange
  • You like the toilet and it flushes well
  • The leak is at the supply line or tank bolts

Replace when

  • The bowl or tank is cracked
  • It clogs repeatedly with normal use
  • Parts are hard to find for an old model
  • You want a more efficient or taller comfort height model

How a toilet installation goes

Installing a new toilet is more involved than it looks. Our plumbing installation team handles the details.

Remove the old toilet

We shut off and drain the water, disconnect the supply and lift the toilet away from the flange.

Check the flange

A flange that is cracked, corroded or set below the floor needs repair or a spacer before the new toilet is set.

Set and seal

We set a new wax or foam seal, level the toilet and tighten the bolts evenly to avoid cracking the porcelain.

Connect and test

We connect a new supply line, flush several times and check every joint for seepage.

Leaks that waste water quietly

A silent toilet leak can send a surprising amount of water down the drain without a sound. If your water bill rises and nothing else has changed, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.
