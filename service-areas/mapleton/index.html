Plumbing in a town that grew by 42 percent

Mapleton counted 11,365 residents in the 2020 census, up 42.4 percent from 7,979 in 2010. Fast growth means a mix of newer homes with modern PEX and PVC plumbing and older homes with copper, galvanized lines or original water heaters that are now past their expected life.

For you, that mix decides what a plumber should look for. A newer house usually fails at a fixture, a valve or a connector. An older one is more likely to leak at corroded fittings or struggle with a main sewer line that has seen decades of roots. We ask about the age of the house on the first call so the right parts are on the truck.

Before we arrive in Mapleton

These steps keep a bad situation from getting worse while we are on the way.

Find the main shutoff

Locate the valve where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, garage or utility room. Turning it clockwise stops water to every fixture.

Stop using drains

If a toilet or tub is backing up, pause the washer and dishwasher and skip showers. Every gallon has nowhere to go.

Switch off power near water

Turn off breakers for wet areas and keep out of standing water near outlets and appliances.

Note what you see

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are affected, how old the house is and whether the pressurized irrigation is running.

Culinary water and pressurized irrigation

Mapleton City reports that its culinary water comes from wells named Carnesecca, Seal, Westwood and Crowd Canyon, plus Mapleton Springs, which was rehabilitated after contamination. Five storage tanks hold more than 10.8 million gallons, and about 500,000 feet of pipe, from 4 to 20 inches, delivers it. The city counted 3,655 culinary connections in 2022.

Pressurized irrigation serves roughly half the city, mainly the north and central areas, and runs about April through October. That matters at the start of the season, because a cracked sprinkler line or an open hose bib can look like a plumbing leak. If water use jumps in spring, close the irrigation supply and watch the meter before assuming the house has a leak. The city Public Works line, 801-489-6253, can answer questions about which system serves your lot.

Local notes for Mapleton

Hobble Creek forms the northern boundary of the city, and the Mapleton Lateral Canal, which the city began piping in 2007, carries irrigation water across the bench. US-89 is the main road through town, and the I-15 corridor lies a few miles west.

Mapleton began sewer collection in 1996 and growth accelerated afterward, so sewer age depends on the neighborhood. Parks such as Mapleton City Park, Ira Allen Park, Eagle Rock Park, North Park and Harvest Park are good landmarks when you give directions. The Pioneer Heritage Museum at 115 S Main is another easy reference point.
