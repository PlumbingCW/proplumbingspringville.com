Three ZIP codes, three kinds of calls

Provo has three main ZIP codes. The 84604 area is the largest by population at 45,503 residents, 84601 has 33,986 and 84606 has 33,791. Many callers give a street and a ZIP, and the ZIP helps us plan.

Home ages vary a lot across all three ZIP codes, so the pipe material question comes first on every call. Older houses are more likely to have aged sewer laterals and galvanized or copper supply lines, and newer ones tend to fail at fixtures and connectors.

Provo plumbing problems by building type

Different buildings fail in different ways.

Single family homes

Water heaters, clogged mains and hidden slab or wall leaks are the common calls.

Rentals and student housing

Toilets, disposals and drains see heavy use, and clogs from wipes and grease are frequent.

Apartment buildings

A shared stack backing up affects several units, so reach the property manager and call us.

Businesses and restaurants

Floor drains and grease lines back up during busy hours. See commercial plumbing.

Before we arrive in Provo

Steps that work in a house or an apartment.

Find the shutoff

In an apartment, the unit shutoff is often under a sink or in a utility closet. If not, ask the manager for the building main.

Stop the source

Turn off the supply valve under a toilet or sink if that is where water is coming from.

Keep drains quiet

Stop the washer, dishwasher and showers if anything is backing up.

Notify the owner

Renters should tell the landlord or property manager right away so approvals do not delay repairs.

Landmarks and roads we plan around

Provo was founded as Fort Utah in 1849 on the east bank of Utah Lake, with the Provo River running through the city. Provo Canyon and US-189 climb east, Y Mountain overlooks the city, and I-15 runs through the western side. BYU enrolls over 30,000 students, and FrontRunner commuter rail has served the city since December 2012.
