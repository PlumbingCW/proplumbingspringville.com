A water system built on springs and wells

Spanish Fork reports that its culinary water comes from five springs and two wells, with total dissolved solids between 272 and 724 ppm in the 2025 water quality report. The 90th percentile lead result was 1 ppb across 30 sites. Its system includes about 270 miles of main serving more than 12,000 connections.

The city does not publish a hardness figure in the report, so test your tap if you are shopping for a softener or worrying about scale. The city's own guidance also warns about cross connections at sprinklers, so a hose left in a pool or a sprinkler without backflow protection is not a harmless thing. For water questions the city lists 801-804-4500.

Before we arrive in Spanish Fork

A few minutes of preparation limits damage.

Shut the main

Close the valve where water enters the house. If it is stuck, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher.

Open a low faucet

After closing the main, open a tap at the lowest point to drain pressure from the lines.

Pause drains

If anything is backing up, stop dishwashers, washers and showers.

Move belongings

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area when it is safe.

Local reference points

Spanish Fork was settled in 1851, incorporated in 1855 and has an Icelandic settlement history. The Thurber School on Main Street dates to 1910, and Fiesta Days in July is the big yearly event. I-15 runs along the northwest side of town, and Payson lies several miles to the southwest.
