Plumber in the Historic District, Springville, UT

The Springville Historic District is the old center of town, the oldest part of town, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in January 2004. Springville Pro Plumbing helps homeowners here with drains, water heaters, leaks and sewer lines.

Houses in this part of Springville were built across more than a century, so one block can hold very different plumbing. Call and tell us the street and what is happening. We will explain the repair options before you decide.

Where the district sits

The city describes the district as running between 400 North and 800 South, and between 400 East and 400 West. Main Street runs through the middle, and the Springville Museum of Art at 126 East 400 South is one of the landmarks inside the boundary. Buildings on both sides of the bounding streets count as part of the district.

Addresses inside that boundary can be very different from one another, so we ask about the house itself before we guess at the problem.

A district with many eras of housing

Springville's design standards for the district count 1,238 primary buildings, of which 897 contribute to its historic character. They group the housing into five types: Vernacular Classical from about 1850, Springville Victorian, Bungalow, Period Revival and the Post War Cottage and Ranch house. The standards say twentieth century styles make up about three quarters of the significant primary buildings.

For a homeowner, that range matters in one practical way. A house that has stood for decades has usually been repaired, added to and updated in stages. The pipe in one wall may not match the pipe in the next, and the age of the building alone does not tell us what is inside it. The only way to know is to look, which is why we start with the exposed lines and, for a sewer problem, a sewer camera inspection.

Before you call about an older house

Older houses hide their shutoffs and cleanouts in odd places. Spend a minute on these before the phone call.

Hunt for the main valve

Look in the basement, a crawl space or a utility corner where the water line comes through the foundation. Turn it clockwise to the end.

Rest the drains

When a tub or toilet gurgles or rises, leave the washer, dishwasher and showers off so the line can settle.

Dry the area and mind the outlets

Cut the breakers for any wet room and keep clear of standing water near plugs and appliances.

Snap a photo and guess the age

A picture of the problem and a rough build year help us picture what is behind the wall.

Local notes for the district

Lots in the original plat vary a great deal. The design standards describe large two story houses on corner lots with about 100 feet of frontage and one story houses on narrower lots, some about 50 feet wide, set back into the lot. That layout can mean a long run from the street to the back of the house, which is worth knowing when a sewer line is involved.

The city's 2024 water report lists total dissolved solids from 240 to 476 ppm and gives no hardness figure. A store test kit on your own tap answers the question before you buy a treatment system. Yard watering comes from the separate pressurized irrigation system, so a soggy lawn can mean a sprinkler line rather than a leak in the house supply.

Permits and exterior changes

Whether a plumbing job needs a permit is a question for Springville's Building Division, reachable at 801-489-2704. If a repair touches the outside of the house, ask Community Development whether the design standards apply before work starts. Those standards were written for new construction and additions, and we cannot say from here how they apply to any particular job.

Code and permit note

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments in Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3, whatever the age of the house. Repairs to older plumbing are expected to meet current code for the work being done.
