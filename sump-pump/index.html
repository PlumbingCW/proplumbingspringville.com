Sump pump emergency: water rising in the basement

The pump is the only thing between groundwater and your floor. When it stops, minutes matter.

Check power first

Look for a tripped breaker or an unplugged cord. Test the outlet. Use a GFCI reset if one is present.

Lift the float

Raise the float switch to see whether the motor starts. If it hums but pumps nothing, the impeller may be jammed or the check valve stuck.

Bail what you can

Use buckets or a wet vacuum if it is safe and the power is off to nearby outlets. Move stored items off the floor.

Call and describe

Tell us whether the pit is full, whether the pump runs and whether the discharge pipe is flowing outside.

Springville rules for sump discharge

Springville's municipal code addresses sumps and discharges. Section 4-13-328 says a sump may not be constructed in the city unless the city has approved it, and section 4-13-202 allows discharges from foundation drains, footing drains and crawl space or basement pumps only when the city has approved them in writing.

Where the discharge goes matters. Before routing a new line, call the Building Division at 801-489-2704 and ask what applies to your address. Water pumped onto a neighbor's lot or into a place the city has not approved can create a code issue.

Hobble Creek, groundwater and why basements get wet

Springville sits between the Wasatch Range and Utah Lake, with Hobble Creek running through the city. The city notes high spring snowmelt flows in 2023 and asks owners to keep creek banks clear of debris, since blockages can push water into areas it should not reach.

The practical approach is to watch your own basement. If it gets damp in spring runoff, a sump pit and pump are inexpensive insurance. See also our basement waterproofing page for permanent fixes beyond a pump.
