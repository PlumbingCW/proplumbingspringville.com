When a failed tank is an emergency

Not every water heater problem needs a replacement, but these four do, and they are worth treating as urgent.

Water leaking from the shell

Fittings and valves can be repaired, but water seeping from the tank body means the steel liner has failed. The tank cannot be patched.

A flooded utility space

Even a slow leak can soak drywall, flooring and stored items within hours. Shut off the water and call before the damage spreads.

Rust and metallic taste

Rust colored hot water along with sediment and a spent anode rod points to a tank that is corroding from the inside.

Repeated repairs

A third service call in a short span is a sign the unit is past the point where repairs pay off.

How a replacement visit works

A same-day swap is realistic when the unit and the space are straightforward. Planned installs follow the same steps, covered further on our water heater installation page.

Assess the old unit

We confirm the failure, check the fuel type, venting or electrical supply and the space, and talk through size and options.

Choose the replacement

You pick between a tank, a hybrid heat pump or a tankless unit, with the trade-offs explained plainly. We do not push the largest option.

Drain, remove and set

We drain the old tank, disconnect it, haul it away and set the new unit with new connectors, a relief valve and discharge line.

Test and walk through

We fire or energize the unit, check for leaks and combustion safety on gas models, set the temperature and show you the shutoffs.

Permits, codes and what to ask the city

Whether a permit applies to a water heater swap depends on the scope, and Springville's published building FAQ does not spell it out. Call the Building Division at 801-489-2704 and ask. Their permit checklist also notes that required plumbing and gas line inspections are scheduled through the City Inspect portal with 24 hours of notice.

Utah licenses plumbers in apprentice, journeyman and master classes through the Division of Professional Licensing, and you can verify any plumber at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html. Ask the same question of anyone you hire.

What a longer tank life really depends on

A replacement tank lasts longer when it is flushed on a schedule and its anode rod is checked. Springville's city report lists total dissolved solids between 240 and 476 ppm for 2024 and no hardness figure, so test your own tap or ask the Water Division at 801-489-2740 before deciding how aggressive a flush routine needs to be.

Ask us about a hybrid unit if the space allows it. Ask your electric and gas providers whether a water heating rebate is currently offered before you buy.

Code and permit note

Code note: a new tank is set up to the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with a relief valve and discharge pipe, and Utah applies the IPC with statewide amendments.
