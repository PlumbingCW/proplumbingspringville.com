Plumbing emergency? Do these four things first

Water and sewage move fast. A few minutes of action limits the damage while your emergency plumber is on the way.

Close the main shutoff

Find the valve where the water line enters the house and turn it clockwise. It stops water to every fixture.

Stop using drains

If a toilet or tub is backing up, pause the washer and dishwasher and skip showers. Every gallon has nowhere to go.

Keep power away from water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms and stay out of standing water near outlets.

Call and describe what you see

Tell the dispatcher the address, which fixtures are affected and how old the house is.

Local conditions

What Springville weather and water mean for your plumbing

Plumbing problems here follow the calendar. Knowing the pattern helps you prevent a call, and helps us fix the cause when you do call.

Cold winters

Springville sits at about 4,554 feet. January brings a mean low near 22°F and a mean high near 40°F, so pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces and garages are at risk. See burst pipe repair.

Spring snowmelt

Hobble Creek ran high from snowmelt in 2023. Groundwater rises with it, so basements and sump pits are tested every April. See sump pump service.

Spring and well water

The city water system draws from four springs and seven wells, and its 2024 report lists total dissolved solids from 240 to 476 ppm. Hardness is not reported, so test your tap before buying a softener.

Permits and inspections

Springville schedules plumbing inspections through its City Inspect portal with 24 hours of notice. The Building Division, 801-489-2704, can confirm what your project needs.

Springville plumbing season guide

What local conditions put pressure on your plumbing.

  • WinterJanuary lows average about 22°F. Frozen and split pipes top the list.
  • SpringSnowmelt raises Hobble Creek and groundwater. Sump pumps work hard and irrigation starts up.
